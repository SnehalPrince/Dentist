Few dental procedures carry as much undeserved fear as the root canal. For many patients, just hearing the words "root canal" triggers anxiety — despite the fact that the procedure is designed to relieve pain, not cause it. Let's look at five of the most common myths and set the record straight.

## Myth 1: Root Canals Are Extremely Painful

**The Truth:** Root canal therapy is performed under local anesthesia. You'll feel pressure and movement, but pain during the procedure is not the norm.

In fact, the pain that typically brings someone to their dentist in the first place — the throbbing, severe toothache caused by an infected pulp — is the actual source of misery. The root canal *treats* that pain. Most patients report that the procedure itself is comparable to getting a filling.

Any discomfort after the procedure is usually mild and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief for a few days.

## Myth 2: It's Better to Just Pull the Tooth

**The Truth:** Saving your natural tooth is almost always the better option.

Your natural teeth are uniquely valuable — they provide the best biting force, are easier to maintain, and help preserve jawbone density. Extracting a tooth sets off a chain of events: neighboring teeth can shift, jawbone can deteriorate over time, and replacing the tooth with an implant or bridge adds cost and additional procedures.

Root canal therapy, when successful, lets you keep your natural tooth for many years.

## Myth 3: Root Canals Cause Illness

**The Truth:** This myth originated from flawed research done nearly a century ago and has been thoroughly debunked by modern science.

Root canal therapy removes infected tissue from inside the tooth and seals the area to prevent reinfection. It does not introduce bacteria to your bloodstream in any meaningful way. Major health organizations, including the American Association of Endodontists, confirm that root canal therapy is safe and not linked to systemic illness.

## Myth 4: If the Tooth Doesn't Hurt, You Don't Need a Root Canal

**The Truth:** A dying or dead tooth doesn't always announce itself with pain.

As a tooth's nerve dies — whether from severe decay or injury — the pain may actually subside. That doesn't mean the infection is gone; it means the nerve tissue is no longer alive to signal it. An X-ray during a routine exam can reveal infection or abscess that has no obvious symptoms. This is one reason regular dental checkups are so valuable.

## Myth 5: Root Canals Require Many Appointments

**The Truth:** Most root canals are completed in one or two appointments.

Simple root canals with straightforward canal anatomy are often finished in a single visit. More complex cases — involving multiple canals or significant infection — may require a follow-up. After the root canal itself, you'll typically return to have a crown placed to protect and restore the treated tooth.

## What to Actually Expect

1. **Anesthesia:** The area is numbed completely before anything begins.
2. **Pulp Removal:** The infected or inflamed tissue inside the tooth's canals is carefully cleaned out.
3. **Sealing:** The canals are disinfected, shaped, and sealed with a material called gutta-percha.
4. **Crown:** A dental crown is placed at a follow-up appointment to protect the treated tooth.

Recovery involves mild soreness for a few days — a small price for a tooth that can now last many more years.

## The Bottom Line

If your dentist recommends a root canal, the procedure is far less intimidating than its reputation suggests. It's a reliable, well-established treatment that saves teeth every day — and most patients leave wondering what they were so worried about.
