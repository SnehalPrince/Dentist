Good oral health is a family affair. The habits your kids build early stay with them for life — and the challenges adults face shift as we age. Here's a guide to what healthy dental care looks like at every stage.

## For Young Children (Ages 0–6)

Dental care starts earlier than most parents expect — even before the first tooth appears.

- **Before teeth:** Wipe your baby's gums with a damp cloth after feedings to reduce bacteria.
- **First tooth:** Begin brushing as soon as it appears, using a rice grain-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste.
- **First dental visit:** Schedule around your child's first birthday, or within six months of their first tooth erupting. Early visits are mostly about familiarizing kids with the dental environment and catching any early issues.
- **Avoid:** Putting children to sleep with a bottle of milk or juice. The sugars pool around teeth and cause "baby bottle tooth decay."

The key goal at this stage is making dental care feel normal and positive — not scary.

## For Older Children (Ages 6–12)

As permanent teeth start coming in, good habits become even more important.

- **Brushing:** Help children brush until around age 7–8, when they have the fine motor control to do it effectively on their own.
- **Flossing:** Start flossing once teeth touch each other — usually around age 2–3 for some teeth, and increasingly important as permanent teeth come in.
- **Sealants:** Ask your dentist about dental sealants — thin protective coatings applied to the chewing surfaces of back teeth to prevent cavities. They're highly effective and painless.
- **Mouthguards:** If your child plays contact sports, a custom-fitted mouthguard protects their teeth far better than over-the-counter options.

## For Teenagers

Teens face unique dental challenges as orthodontic work, diet choices, and busy schedules intersect.

- **Orthodontic hygiene:** Braces make thorough cleaning harder. Invest in an interproximal brush and water flosser to clean around brackets and wires.
- **Diet:** Limit sugary drinks — sports drinks and sodas are some of the biggest drivers of teen cavities. Acidic drinks erode enamel even if they're not sugary.
- **Wisdom teeth:** The late teen years are when wisdom teeth typically start developing. Regular X-rays allow your dentist to monitor their position.
- **Piercings:** Tongue and lip piercings significantly increase the risk of chipped teeth and gum damage. If your teen has one, check for any early signs of damage at dental visits.

## For Adults

Life gets busy, but consistent habits pay long-term dividends.

- **Brush twice, floss once:** This combination removes the vast majority of plaque that causes cavities and gum disease.
- **Watch for gum disease:** Bleeding gums when you brush or floss isn't normal — it's a sign of gingivitis, the early stage of gum disease. It's reversible with professional cleaning and better home care.
- **Don't skip cleanings:** Even with great home care, tartar builds up in areas a toothbrush can't reach. Professional cleanings every six months remove it before it causes damage.
- **Grind your teeth?** Nighttime grinding (bruxism) is common under stress and silently wears down enamel. A custom night guard protects your teeth while you sleep.

## For Older Adults (60+)

Oral health needs shift with age, but good care is always achievable.

- **Dry mouth:** Many medications cause dry mouth, which significantly increases cavity risk. Sip water frequently, chew sugar-free gum, and mention any new medications to your dentist.
- **Root exposure:** As gums recede with age, tooth roots become exposed and are highly susceptible to decay. Fluoride treatments can help protect them.
- **Dentures and partials:** If you wear dentures, clean them daily and remove them at night to let your gums rest.
- **Oral cancer screening:** Regular dental checkups include an oral cancer screening. The earlier it's caught, the better the outcomes.

## Building Habits That Last

The best dental routine is the one you'll actually do consistently. Here are universal tips for every age:

1. **Brush for two full minutes,** twice a day, with a fluoride toothpaste
2. **Floss once a day** — the easiest time to remember is right before bed
3. **Drink plenty of water** — it rinses food particles and helps neutralize acids
4. **Visit your dentist regularly** — most people benefit from a checkup and cleaning every six months

Good oral health isn't complicated. It just requires consistency — and a dental team you trust.
