## What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that's surgically placed into your jawbone to serve as an artificial tooth root. Once it bonds with the bone — a natural process called **osseointegration** — a realistic-looking crown is attached on top.

The result is a tooth replacement that looks, feels, and functions like your natural tooth. You can eat what you want, brush normally, and smile without thinking about it.

## How Do Implants Compare to Other Options?

| Option | Appearance | Function | Bone Preservation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dental Implant** | Excellent | Like natural tooth | Yes — stimulates bone |
| **Dental Bridge** | Good | Good, but removes enamel from adjacent teeth | No |
| **Partial Denture** | Acceptable | Limited, may slip | No |

Implants are generally considered the superior long-term option because they preserve jawbone density, don't affect neighboring teeth, and are the most durable replacement available.

## Are You a Good Candidate?

Most healthy adults are candidates for dental implants. Ideal candidates:

- Are in generally good health
- Have sufficient jawbone density to support the implant
- Have healthy gums
- Do not smoke heavily (smoking significantly slows healing)
- Do not have uncontrolled diabetes or conditions that significantly impair healing

A thorough examination — including X-rays or a 3D scan — is the only way to determine if implants are right for your specific situation.

## The Implant Process: Step by Step

**Step 1: Consultation & Planning**

Your dentist reviews your bone structure, gum health, and overall oral health. If you have insufficient bone volume, a bone graft may be recommended before implant placement.

**Step 2: Implant Placement**

Under local anesthesia, the titanium post is placed into the jawbone. This is a surgical procedure, but most patients manage post-procedure discomfort well with over-the-counter pain relief.

**Step 3: Osseointegration (Healing)**

Over the next several weeks to months, the implant fuses with your jawbone. During this time, you'll have a temporary restoration to maintain function and appearance.

**Step 4: Crown Placement**

Once the implant has fully integrated, a custom-made crown is attached. Your dentist will match the color and shape to your natural teeth for a seamless result.

## Caring for Your Implant

Dental implants are refreshingly low-maintenance:

- **Brush** twice daily just like your natural teeth
- **Floss** daily — specialized implant floss or water flossers work well
- **Attend regular cleanings** — your hygienist will monitor the health of the tissue around the implant
- **Avoid** using your teeth as tools or habitually chewing hard items like ice

## How Long Do Implants Last?

With proper care, the implant post itself can last a lifetime. The crown typically lasts 15–25 years before it may need replacement due to normal wear.

## Cost Considerations

Dental implants typically cost more upfront than alternatives, but their longevity often makes them more cost-effective over the long run compared to bridges that need replacement or dentures that require adjustments and adhesives.

Many patients find that the investment in an implant pays for itself over time — both financially and in quality of life.

## The Bottom Line

Dental implants represent the most natural, durable tooth replacement available. If you're missing a tooth — or facing extraction — an implant consultation is well worth exploring. The best way to know if they're right for you is to have an examination and conversation with your dentist.
